# Manga — ink panels, speed lines, one red stamp

A black-and-white manga page. White paper, heavy ink panel borders laid at slightly irregular sizes and tilts, screentone halftone patches, radial speed lines slicing panel corners, speech bubbles with tails, and giant outlined sound-effect words that overlap the borders. Built for roast, story and plot-twist narration. Energy comes from LINE WEIGHT AND TILT, never from colour or softness — the only colour is red, and most compositions don't even spend it.

## Palette semantics
- Paper and panel are the same white on purpose: a panel is defined by its thick ink border, not by a fill difference. Light grey fills quiet caption boxes only.
- Ink does everything — borders, text, speed lines, halftone dots, and the inverted panel (ink ground, paper type), which is the loudest emphasis available WITHOUT red.
- RED appears zero or one time per composition: a bordered stamp, a circled word, or one sound-effect stroke. Two reds on one page breaks the system; reach for inversion or a bigger SFX first.
- Ink is opaque: tone is faked with halftone dots, never with transparency.

## Layout language
- Panels are never equal and never straight: one dominates, the other yields, each tilted a degree or two, like a page fighting across the gutter.
- Texture is idiomatic: speed-line wedges cut into corners, halftone patches lean out from panel edges, a jagged lightning bolt divides clashing panels.
- Dialogue lives in white elliptical bubbles with tails; the SFX word is giant, outlined in paper-and-ink, rotated, and allowed to cross borders.
- Type is heavy and tight — the boldness is the brush; captions and panel numbers stay small and quiet.

## Signature (miss one and it is off-system)
- The thick ink panel border with its slight tilt — the page's atom; clean straight boxes are off-system.
- Impact hierarchy: quiet cards (one panel, a tone patch) alternate with loud cards (SFX, speed lines), so the slams keep their punch.

## Motion personality
Cuts, not tweens: panels shove in hard from opposite sides, tone patches snap-fade, the SFX and the red stamp land with a heavy press — always last, the impact frame. The page freezes fast, like print.

## Two faces
- Overlaid on footage: floating panels and bubbles inked over the shot — a title banner, a speech bubble, a small bordered panel with tone — each carrying its own white paper fill inside its ink border.
- As a full page: the full spread — unequal panels, a bolt down the seam, one stamp — the strip's whole beat on one page.

## Taboos
A second red. Colour anywhere else. Equal-sized straight panel grids. Gradients or soft light. Transparency tricks. Gentle easing. Emphasis by size-creep instead of inversion.
