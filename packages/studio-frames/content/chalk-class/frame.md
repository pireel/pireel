# Chalkboard — classroom board writing

Classroom board writing (banshu). A deep-green blackboard in a dark wooden frame, chalk-white handwriting energy, dashed chalk boxes sitting slightly rotated, circled numerals ①②③ heading every point, and a hand-drawn underline that overshoots its word at both ends. Built for teaching, how-tos and knowledge breakdowns. Nothing is machine-straight — every box and line carries a tiny tilt, and chalk dust speckles the corners.

## Palette semantics
- The board green is the ground; the dark wood frames it. Chalk does not glow — light here is fluorescent-classroom flat.
- Three chalks only, and the teacher never wastes the coloured ones: WHITE writes the body, YELLOW marks what is on the test (key terms, the circled numerals, the underline, the emphasis ellipse), PINK marks what will hurt you (warnings, and the teacher's corner tag in the voice of 划重点/要考/背下来 — one pink element per composition).
- Faded chalk draws the dashed borders and separators; a few dust dots sit near strokes at low opacity.

## Layout language
- Everything is written, not typeset: boxes are dashed and tilted a fraction of a degree, underlines rotate slightly and overshoot, corner scribbles lean like margin notes. Perfectly straight lines break the illusion.
- Points come numbered: ①②③ lead rows and steps, in yellow, each row tilted differently so the board feels hand-filled.
- One word per composition may be circled by a dashed yellow ellipse — the teacher ringing the takeaway.
- Board writing stays big enough for the back row; nothing shrinks to fine print.

## Signature (miss one and it is off-system)
- The overshooting hand-drawn underline on the key term — machine-cropped underlines are off-system.
- The scarcity economy of the chalks: if everything is highlighted, nothing is. One circled word, one pink element, yellow only on what matters.

## Motion personality
Stroke order: things appear in the order a hand would write them — quick fades with a small rotation settle, underlines drawing from the left, the ellipse circling its word last like the class-dismissal bell. No bouncing, no gliding.

## Two faces
- Overlaid on footage: hand-held slates — a title strip, a corner scorecard, a dashed point box — each on its own small patch of board so the chalk reads, sized like a board the teacher holds up.
- As a full page: the whole board inside its wooden frame, a full lesson's banshu laid out in numbered strokes.

## Taboos
Machine-straight lines and perfectly level boxes. Glow. More than one pink element. Yellow spent on ordinary words. Un-numbered lists. Tiny text the back row can't read. Elastic bouncing.
