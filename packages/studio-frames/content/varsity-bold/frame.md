# Varsity Bold — locker-room broadcast graphics

A stadium broadcast package. Charcoal night, blaze-orange strikes, and EVERYTHING skewed forward — plates, type, tags — so the whole frame leans like a sprinter off the blocks. Giant hollow jersey numbers haunt the background, hazard stripes cut diagonals, and matchups read like a scoreboard. Built for fitness, sports and competitive commentary. Fast, heavy, contested; the sound of the frame is an air-horn sting — if a composition wouldn't fit under one, it is too soft.

## Palette semantics
- Charcoal is the arena dark, moody and mostly empty — density lives in the slabs.
- Chalk white is headline ink; the faded voice belongs to losers and captions.
- Blaze ORANGE is the winner's voice, spent ONCE per composition: the deciding slab, the hazard stripes, the key stat chip. Whatever sits on orange speaks in the arena's dark tone, and only the winner may glow.
- Steel outlines the ghost jersey numbers and secondary labels — never a background fill.
- Structure comes from skew and stripes, not hairlines.

## Layout language
- The skew law: nothing in this frame stands upright — every plate, tag and headline leans forward at the same angle; the italic energy IS the identity.
- Ghost jersey numbers pin to a corner, hollow-outlined and half-cropped, counting up across the video like rounds.
- Comparisons always crown a winner — no neutral ties: the loser sits faded on the left, the winner blazes orange on the right, a bordered VS seam riding the joint.
- Hazard stripe bands bracket the action — a thin sliver to open a stat, a full band to close a section, never two full bands in one frame.
- Headlines are verb-first commands, short and heavy; values wear athletic notation with units in the caption line, never inline.

## Signature (miss one and it is off-system)
- The uniform forward skew on everything — one upright element breaks the sprint.
- One orange statement per composition; steel and charcoal do the rest of the talking.

## Motion personality
Slams in opposed pairs: slabs crash in from opposite sides, chips counter-snap, stripes wipe through — every entrance fast and hard, and the winner fires one quick pulse. Broadcast graphics never dawdle and never bounce.

## Two faces
- Overlaid on footage: lower-third graphics over the broadcast — a skewed title slab, a corner score bug, a stat tag with stripes — never blocking the athlete on camera.
- As a full page: the full graphics frame cuts in — charcoal edge to edge, ghost number, slabs and stripes — like a mid-game stats board.

## Taboos
Upright rectangles or unskewed type. Two orange statements. Pastel anything. Gentle fades. Neutral ties in a comparison. Serif or lightweight type. Units inline with the number.
